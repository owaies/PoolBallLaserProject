X. CHALLENGES ANDLIMITATIONS
Key systemic challenges restricting real-world deployment
include:
A. High Computational and Power Constraints
Running deep semantic segmentors (like U-Net or Mask R-
CNN) on compact single-board devices (such as a Raspberry
Pi) requires significant network quantization and pruning to
achieve acceptable frame rates.
B. Adversarial and Visual Susceptibility
Vision pipelines remain sensitive to practical disturbances.
Moving hands, cue sticks, or changing shadows on the felt
disrupt edge detection and cause false targets in neural classi-
fiers.
C. Data Long-Tail Distribution
Building comprehensive training datasets for edge cases—
such as tightly clustered balls, steep reflection angles, or worn
felt textures—remains labor-intensive and costly.
XI. FUTURERESEARCHDIRECTIONS
To address these challenges, future efforts should focus on:
A. Neuromorphic Edge Computing
Exploring event-driven, spike-based sensors to achieve
ultra-low processing delays (<1ms) and reduced power
consumption, enabling high-speed collision tracking without
motion blur.
B. Foundation Models for Cue Sports
Developing self-supervised vision models capable of trans-
ferring across different pool tables, felt colors, and lighting
setups without manual recalibration.
C. Formal Mechatronic Verification
Combining control barrier functions (CBFs) with neural
trajectory planners to ensure safe, stable operation during high-
speed galvanometer laser steering.
XII. CONCLUSION
This survey has reviewed computer vision, trajectory predic-
tion, and physical guidance methodologies in autonomous cue-
sport systems. By establishing a 5-axis taxonomy, compiling
a 40-paper comparison matrix, and evaluating hardware trade-
offs, this work outlines key progress and persistent limitations
in the field. Resolving existing gaps in light sensitivity, edge
processing delays, and physical projection calibration will be
essential to building practical, low-cost training assistants.
